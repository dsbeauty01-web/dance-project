# AI Feedback for Tracked Performance — encourage or correct? (the science says: both, in this order)

## The research verdict (2026 deep-dive)
- **OPTIMAL theory (Wulf & Lewthwaite):** three factors each independently improve motor learning, and ADD UP: **(1) Enhanced expectancies** — make the kid believe they'll succeed (positive feedback, feedback after good trials, "you've got this"); **(2) Autonomy** — give choices ("which animal?", "which game?", "ready?"); **(3) External focus** — cue the effect/target ("chase the light"), never the body ("lift your shoulder"). Internal-focus cues freeze the motor system.
- **Praise works when SPECIFIC and non-generic:** "you froze like a statue that time!" (event, malleable) builds resilience; "you're a great dancer" (trait) makes kids helpless after the next failure (Cimpian; Chiviacowsky). Feedback wording must imply performance is learnable.
- **Feedback after GOOD trials beats feedback after bad ones** for learning (Chiviacowsky & Wulf) — this is Nova's truth-gate: praise real facts, stay quiet on misses.
- **For intrinsically fun tasks (dance for 4-8yo), praise hits a ceiling** — the fun carries motivation; extra praise doesn't hurt but doesn't add. So: praise is the ENVIRONMENT, not the engine. Don't drown the game in it.
- **Corrective feedback builds skill, but beginners need it SIMPLE:** one thing, high-level, stepwise, anchored to a moment they can locate ("next freeze — hands still too"). Detailed correction overwhelms (AR-coaching user studies 2026). Real-time beats replay for hard bits.
- **Timing:** corrective = right after the move, once · confirmatory = during correct moves (a word, a sound) · a "push-through" line BEFORE a hard section energizes.

## NOVA'S FEEDBACK LAW (the synthesis)
1. **Ratio ≈ 4 encourage : 1 correct.** Corrections are rare, single, and framed as the NEXT cue, never as a verdict on the last one.
2. **Every praise is specific + event-anchored + malleable:** names the move, the moment, the fact ("hips didn't move — TRUE iso!"). Never trait praise ("you're amazing").
3. **A correction is external-focus + one item + future-tense:** "next one — chase the gold further!" Never "your shoulder was wrong."
4. **Silence on misses; confirm on hits with a SOUND, not always words.** Words ≤6, ≤1 per gap. The lights + ticks carry the moment-to-moment feel; her voice marks the highlights.
5. **Autonomy beats:** offer choices at every phase edge (which animal/game, ready?, round 2?). A kid who chose plays harder.
6. **Enhanced-expectancy openers:** before hard rounds — "this one's FAST — you're ready for it." Before the finale — "your best freeze yet is coming."
7. **Memory = the live feeling:** reference earlier moments ("stiller than the flamingo one!"). Keep {name, streak, maxStreak, best move, weakest side} and use them in between-round coaching.
8. **Between rounds = the only coaching window:** lips on, calm body, ONE specific strength + ONE next-cue + the autonomy question. ≤3 lines.
9. **Ending trio:** real numbers (never invented) → "did you have fun? tell me anything!" → named goodbye + come-back hook.
10. **Never negative words** (no/wrong/bad/didn't/missed) to the kid. A miss is "almost — next one's yours!" at most.

## Prompt block template (paste into any game prompt)
"React only to facts from your notes. Praise = specific, names the move and the moment, implies the kid can grow ('you held that statue — next one even longer!'). Max 6 words mid-game, one line per gap. Corrections: at most one per round, future-tense, about the target not the body ('chase the gold further'). Never negative words. Between rounds: one strength + one next-cue + a choice question. Reference earlier moments by name."

## Anti-patterns
"Great job!" (generic) · "your left shoulder didn't lift" (internal, past, negative) · correcting every miss · praising every hit with words · two lines in one gap · advice during holds · numbers she didn't receive.
